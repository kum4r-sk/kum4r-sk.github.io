System services for an isolated, high-renewable grid

Self-directed project, 2026. Built with PyPSA, linopy, HiGHS and Python.

How much does it cost to keep a power grid stable when it can't lean on its neighbours, most of its electricity comes from wind and solar, and transport runs on electricity? And which assets should provide the stability?

To find out, I built a four-zone model of Germany and ran it as an island, with no interconnectors, so that it has to cover every reserve and inertia need itself, as Iceland or Ireland must. It uses real hourly demand, wind and solar data for 2019, scaled to the 2030 national targets, plus a stochastic simulation of 15 million electric cars and 60,000 trucks. On top of PyPSA's standard formulation I wrote custom constraints for linearised unit commitment, upward reserve, and a minimum-inertia limit that battery fast frequency response can partly replace.

What the model shows

  • Stability is expensive when you're on your own. Requiring enough reserve and inertia raises system cost by 17 %. Gas plants kept online for their spinning mass must run at minimum output, which pushes wind and solar off the grid: curtailment rises from 20.6 % to 24.2 %.
  • Batteries remove two thirds of that cost by providing fast frequency response alongside their normal energy trading. What remains is the cost of the spinning inertia that only real machines can supply in the first half second.
  • Smart EV charging is worth more for when it charges than for reserves. Moving charging into hours of surplus wind and solar saves €1.16 bn a year and avoids 6 GW of new gas turbines. EVs pausing to offer reserve adds almost nothing.
  • The results hold at both 1-hour and 3-hour resolution. Scenario differences changed by at most €0.03 bn.

Code and full write-up on GitHub

Limitations, stated in the write-up: four zones with no loop flows; stability approximated by reserve and rate-of-change limits rather than dynamic simulation; one weather year. A natural next step is a frequency-nadir constraint and a real network model of an island grid.

Electrolyte flow in a redox flow cell

Built a 3D CFD model of a laboratory flow-battery cell in STAR-CCM+, treating the carbon-felt electrode as a porous medium, and validated it against pressure-loss measurements on a test rig I designed and commissioned. After finding and correcting a geometry error, the model matched the measurements within about 4 % across all electrode compressions, using only datasheet permeability. Processed multi-day logs from the rig in Python, which traced erratic data to pump pulsation and a sensor offset.
